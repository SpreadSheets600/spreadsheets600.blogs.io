Last time I opened these pages feels distant now, so far back that I cannot even place when. That space between entries grew without me noticing. Recently, something shifted off-grid. Nothing dramatic, nothing loud, just this quiet imbalance. I cannot really name the cause, but there was confusion and no sense of direction. This keeps happening with me now and then.

Later on, I realised I had spent most days either in front of books or playing games. Somehow both at once, which feels weird even saying it. Books stayed open while screens kept glowing. That is rare for me because usually this kind of “study mode” only appears right before exam panic begins. ( Classic me, honestly. )

Yep, exams happened. Two of them, actually, which kept me tied up for a while. Even after they were over, I was still moving around, handling random stuff, jumping between tasks, drifting here and there. Projects? Barely touched. And those projects really needed to be done back then. My project list kept staring at me like I murder someone. The guilt of not doing much productive was settling in. ( Common shit, keeps on happening. I don’t know why I got so dramatic about this. )

After I was done with my first exam I had to go to my grandpa’s house because this was the first election after I turned 18, so I had to vote. Come on, that was on my checklist. I had to do it, and I did. The experience was okay. It was not some dramatic moment like, “Oh my God, I voted, democracy is alive because of me.” Nothing like that. I went, I voted, and came back. Still, one life achievement unlocked, I guess. ( Yea, when I was kid I thought I would never give a vote. I really have no idea what spanned in me XD )

After that, everything turned back toward books because another test was creeping up in a couple of days. Once that ended, I drifted out of sight again. Gaming took over completely for a few days, as if I had quit life itself. The outside world? Updates? Tasks? Duties? Everything went into my backlog. I could not even tell what was happening. All I cared about was just getting over it. ( No I was not playing that, never in my life I will touch that. Do not stare at me like that. )

Then, out of nowhere, a switch flipped somewhere deep inside ( Again this is a common shit for me, my childhood friends do know about it very well ). One random moment, and suddenly I think, fine, this is where life turns around again. Slowly, I started getting back to projects, practical writing, pending work, and all that stuff. I also kind of used you a lot during that phase. Not only that, but college was about to open in a few days, so I became busy with all the pending things I had ignored. Also, my end term was coming up, so I had to get moving. I need to score good marks. Things slowly started passing through my mind again.

But then, a day back, I was randomly scrolling and saw a someone mentioning the anime _Nana_. It was just floating there. Still, something about it stuck. I do not even know why, but it made me want to watch it.

So I started watching _Nana_.

Truthfully, it turned out good. Actually, a bit too good. ( Not fully joking here. ) Things opened up gently. At first, everything seemed warm, heartfelt, visually smooth, almost like a quiet embrace. I believed it. I thought this could be a solid show. Maybe touching, maybe intense, but still something wholesome enough.

Yet it kept moving forward.

And going.

And going.

Out of nowhere, the anime stopped feeling like entertainment. It felt more like watching something fragile break, bit by bit. Just a heads-up: skip what comes next unless you have seen _Nana_ or enjoy heavy themes. This show might leave you unsatisfied and uncomfortable, truth be told.

At first, the show was all good and wholesome until the moment that genuinely made me break a little. What really got to me was not only that Shoji and Nana split, but the mess inside Shoji when it happened. The moment he walked away from Nana stuck with me. His feelings did not make sense, maybe not even to himself.

Here is the thing. Shoji could not stop thinking about Nana. Earlier, he had straight up told Junko that he would lie beside Nana in a grave without hesitation. Words like that do not come from nowhere. When someone says something that intense so calmly, it means something. It was not just passing emotion. It ran deep.

And that is what made it worse.

Because suddenly it felt like he had already checked out on the inside. He had talked like he was ready to marry Nana, to build days with her, to stay even after everything ended. That kind of promise felt real at the time. Yet somehow, his grip on Sachiko stayed firm too. Even as doubt crept in, Sachiko remained close. While one part of him pulled away, another part held tight. Letting go felt impossible, and everything started going wrong right there.

Deep down, his bond with Nana was strong. But Sachiko offered a quieter kind of affection, something gentler and closer. Where Nana left gaps, Sachiko filled them without much effort. Feeling seen like that tilted things. The choice was not loud or dramatic. It simply leaned her way.

That hit me.

Love and obsession are not the same thing after all. What Shoji felt for Nana seemed genuine enough. Pretending it meant nothing feels wrong. Saying you would share a grave with someone is not an idle phrase. Yet his emotions were tangled with fixation, regret, comfort, isolation, and maybe routine by then.

Sometimes feelings feel real even when they are only half true. Not every choice hides inside a grand gesture. Staying clear about who you belong to matters more than anything. Respect shows up in actions that do not waver. Honesty does not bend just because lying hurts less. Being split between two people slowly wears down trust.

Shoji never managed that balance well at all.

And honestly, that was just the beginning.

At that point, I still thought maybe the show would turn around. Maybe things would become wholesome again. Life has ups and downs, right? People break, people heal, people mess up, then somehow things get better.

But no.

From there, the show started sinking deeper and deeper into the dark side. Not immediately, like some cheap drama, but slowly. That was the scary part. Every character started carrying something heavier. Everyone had dreams, but none of those dreams came clean. Love, music, fame, friendship, comfort, sex, loneliness, jealousy, everything started mixing together until it became hard to tell what was right anymore.

At this point, I should probably give a small rundown of what kind of characters this show gave us, because that is where the real damage starts.

We have Nana Komatsu, or Hachi, who begins as this sweet, emotional, slightly dumb-in-love kind of person. Not dumb or anything in a bad way, but more like someone who wants love so badly that she keeps walking straight into problems with full confidence. All she wanted was comfort, romance, attention, and that feeling of being chosen. At first, she feels light and cute, almost like the wholesome part of the show. But slowly, her life becomes one complicated mess after another. The choices she made make her and the people around her pull her into darker places than expected.

Then there is Nana Osaki, the other Nana, who is totally completely different. She looks strong, cool, independent, and untouchable. She is the vocalist of BLAST, and music is the one thing she truly holds onto. But behind all that confidence, she has her own fears. She is scared of being abandoned, scared of being replaced, and scared of losing the people she loves like a little cat. On the matter of fact she was called “Queen Cat” in the show itself. Her pain is quieter at first, but it is always there under the surface.

Then we have Shoji, who starts as someone important in Hachi’s life, but eventually becomes the first major emotional damage dealer. His breakup with Hachi was the first moment where the show made it clear that this was not going to be all wholesome anime. He had feelings, sure, but he was also weak, confused, and unable to choose properly until he had already hurt someone badly. Look I don’t mind this, I am ok with breakups and all. But this kinds hit me hard.

Sachiko enters as the person Shoji chooses, and honestly, that whole thing is messy. She is not written like a cartoon villain, which makes it worse. She is soft, present, and gives Shoji the kind of comfort he wanted. Her character was kind of wierd honestly. But as much as I could understand that she was the one who truly loved Shoji than Nana, yes I am begin serious. But I still felt bad about Nana.

Nobu feels like the guy who genuinely cares, especially when it comes to Hachi. He has that warmer, more honest kind of love, and because of that, it hurts more when things do not go his way. He is not perfect either, but compared to some people in this anime, he feels like emotional first aid. Sadly, this show does not believe in letting emotional first aid work properly. He had his own suffering which he had to deal with, the weight he carried at the end was way more that what was shown and that makes the show even more broken.

Then there is Takumi, and that is where the anime really starts entering dangerous territory. He is charming, successful, powerful, and controlling as hell. IN OTHER WORDS I NEVER SAW A BITCH ASS GUY LIKE HIM. DUDE HE LITERALLY RUINED TWO ( ACTUALLY THREE ) FUCKING LIVES AND WENT ON LIVING LIKE NOTHING HAPPENED. GOD WHY THE FUCK AM I EVEN TAKING ABOUT HIM.

Ren is deeply connected to Nana Osaki. Their relationship has love, but not the peaceful kind. It is intense, emotional, and heavy. They love each other, but there is also distance, pride, fear, and this constant feeling that something might break. Ren is part of Nana’s heart, but also part of the pain she keeps carrying.

And then there are people like Yasu, Junko, Kyosuke, Shin, and the rest, who make the world feel more real. Some give advice, some watch the disaster happen, and some carry their own quiet problems. On another note the character I liked the most was Yasu, he was the real goat. His presence made it feel like calm and controlled, more like anything that happens he will fix it Yasu especially feels like the calm person in the middle of the storm, but even he cannot fix everything. That is the thing with this anime. Even the sensible people are not enough to stop the damage.

So basically, the show starts with characters who look simple enough. Two girls named Nana meet by chance, become roommates, become close, and it feels like this could be a story about friendship, love, music, and growing up.

But slowly, every character becomes heavier.

Each one has their own weight to carry. It feels like watching a group of misfits come together, only to suffer more and more.

Hachi goes from cute romantic chaos to pregnancy, painful choices, and emotional dependence. Nana Osaki goes from cool rockstar energy to fear, loneliness, and abandonment issues. Shoji goes from boyfriend material to disappointment package. Nobu goes from hope to heartbreak. Takumi goes from attractive successful guy to walking red flag with good hair. Ren goes from romantic figure to another piece of Nana’s emotional damage.

And that is how the show gets you.

It does not throw darkness at you all at once. It introduces people like normal characters, lets you get attached, and then slowly shows how broken, selfish, scared, or helpless they can be.

That is honestly worse.

Because by the time things go dark, you already care.

Nana Osaki looked strong, independent, cool, almost untouchable. But inside, she was scared of being left behind. Her relationship with Ren had that intense “I need you, but I also cannot fully depend on you” kind of feeling. It was romantic, yes, but also suffocating at times. The show kept showing how even strong people can be full of fear, and how love can feel less like peace and more like holding onto something before it disappears.

Then there was Hachi. At first, she felt like this sweet, chaotic, emotional girl who just wanted to love and be loved. She was messy, but in a human way. Later, the choices around her became darker. Her relationship with Takumi, her confusion, her pregnancy, and the way everything changed after that made the story feel really heavy.

The pregnancy part especially changed the whole mood. It was no longer just about romance or heartbreak. Suddenly, it became about responsibility, future, family, pressure, and choosing between different kinds of pain. Hachi had to make decisions that were not simple at all. Nobu loved her, or at least cared for her in a softer and more honest way. Takumi, on the other hand, was powerful, controlling, and complicated as hell. Yet because of the pregnancy and everything around it, things started moving in a direction that felt painful and almost impossible to stop.

That is where the show became properly dark for me.

It was not just “someone cheated” or “someone broke up.” It became this whole mess of people hurting each other, sometimes knowingly, sometimes because they were weak, sometimes because they were scared, and sometimes because they did not know what the hell they were doing.

And then the divisions started.

Friend groups changed. Relationships changed. The two Nanas, who once felt like the emotional center of the whole show, slowly started feeling separated by life itself. Not because they hated each other, but because life kept pulling them in different directions. That somehow felt worse. When people fight, at least there is something clear. But when people just drift apart because life becomes too complicated, that hurts differently.

The ending, or the way the story leaves things, also did not give me the clean closure I wanted. It had that unfinished, painful feeling. Like something important broke, and even if people kept living, things would never return to how they were before.

It left me with that annoying “what if” feeling.

What if Hachi had chosen differently? What if Nana had opened up more? What if Shoji had not been weak? What if Nobu had gotten his chance? What if Takumi was not Takumi? What if Nana and Hachi had stayed closer? What if everyone just had one normal conversation before ruining their lives?

But no.

Apparently communication was illegal in this anime.

That is probably why it hit me so much. _Nana_ does not become dark because it suddenly turns edgy or tries too hard to shock you. It becomes dark because everything that happens feels painfully human. People love each other, but still fail each other. People want comfort, but choose the wrong place to find it. People dream, but those dreams slowly start demanding pieces of them back.

The worst part is that nobody feels completely fake. Nobody is purely good. Nobody is purely evil. They are just people. Broken people, confused people, selfish people, lonely people. Somehow that makes it hurt more because you cannot simply point at one person and say, “Yes, you are the whole problem.” Everyone is a little bit of the problem. Everyone is carrying something. Everyone is trying to survive in their own messy way.

The ending did not really give me peace either. It left that hollow feeling, like something important had slipped away and no one knew how to bring it back. The two Nanas, who once felt so close, slowly ended up separated by life, choices, timing, and pain. Not in some loud dramatic way, but in that quiet way where people drift apart and you only realize it after the distance has already become too much.

That kind of ending hurts differently. Because it does not feel like a clean full stop. It feels like unfinished grief. Like a memory that still has warmth in it, but also something sharp underneath.

So yeah, _Nana_ was good. Really good.

But also, what the hell was that emotional damage?

It started as a stylish anime about two girls with the same name meeting by chance. It looked like it would be about friendship, music, romance, and growing up. Then slowly, it turned into heartbreak, obsession, pregnancy, toxic love, broken dreams, emotional dependence, abandonment issues, and people drifting apart like life itself had something against them.

And honestly, I think that is why it stayed with me. It did not break me with one big tragic moment. It kept breaking small things one by one. A relationship here. A friendship there. A dream somewhere else. A little hope, then another little hope.

By the end, I was not even shocked anymore. I was just sitting there like, okay, so this show really came for my peace personally.

I do not regret watching it. That would be a lie. It was worth watching. But I also cannot act like it left me normal. Some stories end and you move on. _Nana_ ended, or rather stopped, and somehow it still kept going inside my head.

This was not just dark stuff. This was dark stuff with good writing. And that is the dangerous kind.
