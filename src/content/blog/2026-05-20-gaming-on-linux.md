### Linux Mint \~ My first home

I started with Linux Mint. And honestly ? Mint deserves respect.

People love clowning beginner distros sometimes, but Mint genuinely made Linux approachable for me. It was stable, simple, clean, and most importantly it did not try to fight me every five minutes.

[IMAGE HERE — Linux Mint desktop on old laptop]

Back then I was not even thinking about gaming properly. That laptop barely qualified as a gaming machine anyway. My entire focus was mainly browing, programming and leraning linux.

And Mint worked beautifully for that.

Everything felt lighter. Faster. Cleaner.

Suddenly the machine that struggled under Windows felt usable again.

That honestly shocked me.

And that was probably the first time Linux clicked in my head.

# Then Hyprland Started Fighting Me

Now here is the thing nobody tells you initially.

Tiling window managers are amazing...

until they suddenly are not.

Hyprland automatically tiles windows dynamically. Which sounds cool until you suddenly open:

\* Discord

\* browser

\* game

\* Spotify

\* terminal

\* documentation

and your screen starts looking like abstract geometry.

Especially after 3+ windows.

That is where my brain started suffering.

But the bigger issue?

Multi-monitor support.

I had two monitors.

And the way Hyprland handled workspaces annoyed me massively.

If I switched workspace on one monitor, the second monitor workspace behavior also changed in ways I did not want. It completely broke my mental workflow.

That became the final reason I switched.

# Wine Is Insane

Wine is probably one of the craziest software projects ever made.

Wine basically reimplements Windows APIs so Linux can understand what Windows applications are trying to do.

Meaning:

the game thinks it is talking to Windows.

But underneath?

Linux is translating everything.

And somehow it works.

Not through emulation.

Not through virtual machines.

Actual compatibility translation.

Which is honestly ridiculous engineering.

[IMAGE HERE — Wine architecture diagram]

Then came Proton.

And Proton basically changed Linux gaming forever.
